Set shape

A slot at one set is a gesture; a fourth set stacked on an accessory isolation is usually better spent on a second movement. Both are corrected by rule — a floor of 2 sets and a cap of 3 on accessory isolations — with exemptions for plans whose identity depends on the exception.

The 4+ non-lead column counts every slot after the day's first carrying four or more sets. The cap only trims the isolation ones — a 4-set compound after the opener is a programming choice, not junk volume, which is why Tenfold's 10×10 blocks and Purgatorio's paired compounds survive.

Flags is the standing review check: every slot at one set or above three, minus the ones a plan’s own identity explains. All remaining flags are second compounds at four sets — a programming judgement call, not junk volume. Run it directly with npx tsx scripts/review-flags.ts.

Exempt from the floor: Blackout (one work set is the plan), Neural Overload (the 1-6 PAP singles are the mechanic), the four legacy powerlifting plans (ME, AMRAP and top singles), and Iron Clock (parked). Timed and density blocks are skipped everywhere — REDLINE's finishers are not slots with a missing second set. Exempt from the cap: each specialisation plan, on its own muscle.

Does each plan do what it says?

Three checks against declarations the codebase already makes: the specialisation groups in PLAN_RULES, and the fatigue rating and experience range in portfolio.ts. Fatigue is banded against the catalogue's own spread across three views — weekly systemic, systemic per session, and axial per session — because a three-day plan with brutal sessions is not a light plan.

How this was measured

Each plan's representative training week is materialised straight from PLAN_REGISTRY — generators run, hooks applied — then every slot is resolved against the exercise library and scored using the same exerciseIntelligence ratings the audit used. The method reproduces the audit's own published figures exactly where they overlap: Athena at 61 sets, 103 systemic, 49 axial, 1.69 per set.

The after-state applies scripts/v2-change-map.ts, which encodes every vote that moves a measured number, each citing its vote id. Votes about wiring, tempo, RIR, progression and dashboards are real work but invisible to a volume model, so they are recorded rather than faked into the arithmetic.

One correction worth naming. An earlier pass credited a 3-set row with six sets of back, because back aggregates lats, upper back, traps and lower back and a row lists two of them as primary. Quads and hamstrings are single-muscle groups and never inflate, so every multi-muscle group looked dominant. Each major group is now counted once per exercise. This reversed two findings: Hamstring Foundry and Workhorse both hold their specialisation, and King of the Squat is led by glutes rather than back. The same defect is live in src/lib/volumeAnalysis.ts, which is what verify:volume uses to gate new plans.

Known limits: the sample is one steady-state week, so ramps and deloads are described but not scored. Trinary and Ritual generate accessory work outside the template, so their figures are the barbell spine only. Super Mutant is measured at 5 sessions/week from a 4–6 range. Adventure is the expected value over all reachable pair routes, not one route. Where a vote made a slot a picker, the default is scored and the alternatives counted separately.
