# Hamstring Foundry

Ten weeks, four days a week, of hamstring specialisation built on one premise:
the muscle gets trained three times weekly through three different functions,
and all three must progress. Heavy hip extension is the barbell RDL (4x5–8,
3.5-minute rests, double progression at 5 kg). Knee flexion is the seated curl.
Lengthened control is the hip-supported dumbbell deadlift, with an explicit
ladder for when the 50 kg dumbbell ceiling arrives: 3s eccentric, then 4s, then
a pause in the stretch, then 1.5 reps, then more reps.

Day 3 is deliberately upper-dominant with no hard hamstring work — it exists to
protect recovery between the specialised lower sessions, and day 4 back-fills
second weekly exposures for chest and quads so the upper day is not the only
one.

Two phases. Forging (weeks 1–5) is accumulation: every curl slot gets a
four-second eccentric, because the eccentric is the exercise. Tempering
(weeks 6–10) removes the tempo, drops curls to 6–10 reps, and pushes the RDL to
5x4–6 — the intensification half of a classic accumulation/intensification
split.
