# Neural Overload

Nine weeks of powerbuilding, four days a week, built around the Poliquin 1-6
method. Each neural day runs the same four-slot pattern on its anchor lift: a
heavy single, a six-rep back-off, a heavier single, then a second back-off six
heavier than the first. The point is post-activation — the second six should
move better than the first because the heavier single primed the nervous
system. The pattern is written out as four explicit slots rather than a
technique so each exposure carries its own percentage.

The singles are prescribed at 90% and 92.5%, never as weekly max attempts, and
the back-offs at 75% and 77.5%. Bench, squat, and weighted chin-up each get a
neural day; paused lifts keep their competition pause and every exposure is
explosive on the way up by construction. Day 4 deliberately breaks the
pattern: front-squat straight sets at 65% for 3–5 reps plus hypertrophy work,
because forcing literal 1-6 loading onto everything costs more neural budget
than it returns.

Phases are Charge (weeks 1–3), Discharge (weeks 4–6), and Overload (weeks
7–9). Overload strips one set from every slot with three or more sets, so
accessory volume falls while the 1-6 work stays untouched and stays the
priority.
