# Apex Predator — Movement Access Assessment Guide

Step-by-step instructions for the six-region access screen used by Apex
Predator at weeks 0, 4, 8 and 12, plus the optional squat screen. Each test
takes under two minutes and needs no specialist equipment.

This is a pattern-access screen, not a medical diagnosis. Any test can be
skipped; a test that produces pain is stopped and recorded as pain, not
scored. Fewer than three valid regions defaults the plan's emphasis to ankle
and thoracic rotation.

Each section ends with image prompts for illustrating that step — one image
per numbered step, generated from the prompt verbatim.

---

## 1. Ankle dorsiflexion (knee-to-wall)

**Measures:** how far the knee can travel over the toes before the heel lifts.
**Unit:** centimetres. Test both sides.
**Scoring:** under 8 cm scores 1, 8–10 cm scores 2, over 10 cm scores 3.

1. Stand facing a wall in a split stance, the test foot flat on the floor,
   toes about a hand's width from the wall.
2. Keep the heel glued down and the knee tracking over the second toe. Drive
   the knee forward toward the wall.
3. If the knee touches the wall easily, slide the foot back and repeat until
   you find the greatest distance where the knee touches without the heel
   rising or the arch collapsing.
4. Measure from the tip of the big toe to the wall. Record that number.

- Step 2: "Close side-view of a lower leg doing a knee-to-wall ankle test:

- Step 4: "Top-down photo of a foot beside a wall with a tape measure running

## 2. Active straight-leg raise (hip flexion)

**Measures:** active hip flexion with the knee locked, without the low back
flattening into the floor.
**Unit:** degrees (estimate against the reference angles below). Both sides.
**Scoring:** under 70° scores 1, 70–85° scores 2, over 85° scores 3.

1. Lie on your back, legs straight, toes of the resting leg pointed up.
2. Brace lightly and lift the test leg with the knee straight, as high as you
   can under control. No swinging, no bend in the knee.
3. Judge the angle of the lifted leg against the floor: vertical is 90°,
   halfway is 45°. A phone camera propped at hip height makes this easy.
4. Record the angle, and note any side difference larger than about 10°.

**Image prompts:**

- Step 2: "Side-view photo of a person lying supine on a gym mat performing
  an active straight-leg raise, one leg flat with toes up, the other leg
  lifted straight to roughly 80 degrees, arms relaxed by the sides."
- Step 3: "Diagram-style photo from the side showing a supine straight-leg
  raise with faint overlaid angle arcs at 45, 70 and 90 degrees from the
  floor, clean minimal background."

## 3. Hip rotation (seated 90/90)

**Measures:** comfortable internal and external rotation at the hip.
**Unit:** degrees. Both sides.
**Scoring:** under 30° scores 1, 30–40° scores 2, over 40° scores 3.

1. Sit on the floor in the 90/90 position: front shin pointing forward with
   the knee bent 90°, back shin out to the side, also bent 90°.
2. Sit tall without bracing your hands behind you. Let the back knee sink
   toward the floor.
3. The score is the angle of the back thigh away from its starting line —
   use the floor tiles or a phone inclinometer app against the thigh.
4. Switch sides and repeat. Record both.

**Image prompts:**

- Step 1: "Three-quarter view photo of a person sitting on a gym floor in a
  90/90 hip position: front leg bent 90 degrees with shin forward, rear leg
  bent 90 degrees with shin to the side, torso upright, hands lightly on the
  floor for balance."
- Step 3: "Top-down diagram photo of the 90/90 seated hip position with a
  faint 40-degree arc drawn between the rear thigh and its starting line."

## 4. Shoulder flexion (standing wall reach)

**Measures:** overhead reach without the ribs flaring.
**Unit:** degrees. Both arms together is fine.
**Scoring:** under 150° scores 1, 150–170° scores 2, over 170° scores 3.

1. Stand with your back, head and hips against a wall, feet a half-step out.
2. Keeping the ribs down (no arching off the wall), sweep both arms overhead
   like a jumping jack in reverse.
3. Judge the angle of the upper arms relative to the floor: arms pointing
   straight up and past the ear line is 180°. Stop where the low back would
   have to arch to go further.
4. Record the angle at that honest endpoint.

**Image prompts:**

- Step 2: "Side-view photo of a person standing with back and head against a
  plain wall, both arms swept overhead to roughly 160 degrees, ribs tucked,
  neutral spine, gym wall background."
- Step 3: "Side-view diagram photo of the same wall reach with faint angle
  arcs at 150, 170 and 180 degrees measured from the floor to the upper arm."

## 5. Shoulder rotation (external rotation reach)

**Measures:** external rotation with the elbow supported at shoulder height.
**Unit:** degrees. Both sides.
**Scoring:** under 60° scores 1, 60–80° scores 2, over 80° scores 3.

1. Stand or sit tall, elbow lifted to shoulder height and bent 90°, palm
   facing down, forearm pointing forward.
2. Without letting the elbow drift or the trunk twist, rotate the forearm up
   toward vertical, like a slow-motion high five.
3. Judge the forearm's angle from its starting (forward) position: vertical
   is 90°.
4. Record the angle each side, and note any difference over about 10°.

**Image prompts:**

- Step 1: "Front-view photo of a person with the right elbow at shoulder
  height, elbow bent 90 degrees, forearm pointing forward with palm down,
  ready to test shoulder external rotation, neutral background."
- Step 2: "Front-view photo of the same shoulder rotation test with the
  forearm rotated up to vertical, elbow still at shoulder height, torso
  still, with a faint 80-degree arc from start to finish position."

## 6. Thoracic rotation (open book)

**Measures:** upper-back rotation independent of the hips.
**Unit:** degrees. Both sides.
**Scoring:** under 35° scores 1, 35–50° scores 2, over 50° scores 3.

1. Lie on your side, hips and knees bent to 90°, arms straight out in front
   with palms together.
2. Keeping the knees pinned together and on the floor, open the top arm like
   a book, following the hand with your eyes, until the shoulder or arm stops
   moving toward the floor behind you.
3. Judge the angle the arm has travelled: flat behind you is 180° from start;
   the useful read is how far the top shoulder rotates relative to the
   pelvis — a partner or a phone video from above helps.
4. Record the angle, both sides.

**Image prompts:**

- Step 1: "Side-view photo of a person lying on their left side on a mat,
  hips and knees bent to 90 degrees, both arms extended forward with palms
  together, the start of an open-book thoracic rotation stretch."
- Step 2: "Overhead photo of the open-book stretch mid-rep: knees stacked and
  pinned, top arm opened toward the floor behind the torso, head turned to
  follow the hand."

## Squat screen (optional, free text)

Film one set of five bodyweight squats from the front and the side. Note what
you see in plain words — heels rising, knees caving, depth loss, torso pitch.
This is recorded as free text (`squatScreen`) and never scored numerically.

**Image prompt:**

- "Two-panel photo: left panel front view, right panel side view, of a person
  at the bottom of a bodyweight squat with heels flat and knees tracking over
  the toes, phone-on-tripod framing, plain gym background."
