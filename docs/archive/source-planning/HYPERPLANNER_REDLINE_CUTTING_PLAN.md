# REDLINE
## HyperPlanner General Cutting Plan

**Status:** Training-design baseline before implementation specification  
**Primary goal:** preserve muscle and strength during a calorie deficit while increasing training density and whole-body energy demand  
**Duration:** 8 weeks  
**Frequency:** 4 days/week  
**Format:** Full Body ×4  
**Session length target:** ~40–50 minutes  
**Working-set target:** ~12–14 conventional work sets + 5–8 minute finisher  
**Audience:** intermediate trainees who already know basic gym movements and want a structured all-round cutting phase

---

# 1. Core Identity

> **Keep the iron heavy. Make every minute count.**

REDLINE is not a "light weights for high reps" cutting plan.

It is built around three principles:

1. **Preserve meaningful load intensity**
2. **Reduce total lifting volume**
3. **Increase training density and whole-body work rate**

The program should feel fast and aggressive without turning the strength work into sloppy cardio.

REDLINE is the general-purpose cutting counterpart to Kali.

## Kali
- more specialized;
- glute + lat biased;
- one systemically demanding compound per day;
- lower-fatigue assistance;
- late specialization intensifiers;
- heavily integrated with Athena/previous strength data.

## REDLINE
- gender-neutral;
- balanced whole-body emphasis;
- full body every session;
- high-density supersets;
- large-muscle exercise pairings;
- short conditioning finishers;
- performance-retention + density progression.

---

# 2. Weekly Structure

Four full-body sessions:

1. **PRESSURE**
2. **REDLINE**
3. **FURNACE**
4. **AFTERBURN**

Every workout contains three layers:

## Layer I — THE ANCHOR

One primary strength movement.

Typical prescription:

**2–3 sets × 3–6 reps @ ~1–2 RIR**

Rest:

**2–4 minutes as needed**

Purpose:

- preserve high-force production;
- preserve technical familiarity with heavier loading;
- maintain a meaningful strength signal during the cut.

The Anchor is **not** rushed.

---

## Layer II — THE BURN

Two or three dense non-competing exercise pairings.

Typical prescription:

**2 sets/exercise**

Rep ranges:

- compounds/stable machines: **6–10 or 8–12**
- isolation: **10–15**
- selected simple movements: **12–20**

Rest:

- roughly **45–75 sec between rounds**
- enough to maintain exercise quality

Purpose:

- retain hypertrophy stimulus;
- increase training density;
- involve large amounts of muscle mass;
- keep session duration controlled.

---

## Layer III — THE FINISHER

A short work-capacity block.

Duration:

- Week 1–2: **5 min**
- Week 3–4: **6 min**
- Week 5: **7 min**
- Week 6–7: **8 min**
- Week 8: optional **5 min benchmark** or omitted if fatigue is high

Preferred movement qualities:

- simple;
- low technical complexity;
- relatively low injury risk under fatigue;
- large-muscle involvement;
- little setup time.

Preferred examples:

- kettlebell swings;
- farmer carries;
- suitcase carries;
- step-ups;
- reverse lunges;
- goblet squats;
- push-ups;
- inverted rows;
- sled work if available;
- bike/rower only as optional equipment-based substitutes.

The finisher must **never grow beyond 8 minutes**.

Progression happens inside the time cap.

---

# 3. Weekly Volume Philosophy

REDLINE deliberately uses moderate/low lifting volume.

Approximate direct/effectively-primary weekly work:

| Muscle | Target |
|---|---:|
| Quads | 8–10 sets |
| Hamstrings | 6–8 sets |
| Glutes | 8–10 sets |
| Back/Lats | 10–12 sets |
| Chest | 7–9 sets |
| Delts | 6–8 sets |
| Biceps | 3–4 direct sets + pulling |
| Triceps | 3–4 direct sets + pressing |
| Calves | 2–4 sets |
| Abs | 2–4 sets |

Frequency remains high:

- chest: 3–4 exposures;
- back/lats: 4 exposures;
- quads: 3–4 exposures;
- hamstrings/glutes: 3–4 exposures;
- delts: 3–4 exposures.

The plan reduces **set volume**, not frequency.

# 8. Exercise-Order Rule

REDLINE alternates muscle regions deliberately.

Avoid:

1. Hack Squat
2. RDL
3. Bulgarian Split Squat

at the start of one workout.

Preferred:

1. Anchor
2. Upper movement
3. Lower movement
4. Upper movement
5. Lower/isolation
6. Small work
7. Finisher

The goal is to accumulate whole-body work without destroying one region early.

# 9. Program Phases

## Weeks 1–2 — IGNITION

Goal:

- establish loads;
- learn the density format;
- avoid excessive fatigue.

### Anchor
**2 RIR**

### Burn work
**2–3 RIR**

### Finisher
**5 minutes**

Do not progress finisher load aggressively yet.

---

## Weeks 3–5 — BURN

Goal:

- preserve/improve anchor performance;
- gradually increase work density.

### Anchor
**1–2 RIR**

### Burn work
**1–2 RIR**

### Finisher

- Week 3: 6 min
- Week 4: 6 min
- Week 5: 7 min

Selected isolation movements may finish around 1 RIR.

---

## Weeks 6–7 — REDLINE

Goal:

- highest density phase;
- maintain heavy anchor performance;
- push low-risk local effort.

### Anchor
**1–2 RIR**

No forced grinders.

### Burn work
**~1 RIR**

### Low-risk final isolation sets
May reach:

**0–1 RIR**

Examples:

- lateral raise;
- leg curl;
- leg extension;
- rear delt;
- lat prayer.

### Finisher
**8 minutes**

Do not extend beyond 8.

---

## Week 8 — ASHES

Goal:

- reduce accumulated fatigue;
- compare performance against baseline.

Reduce Burn/accessory volume by approximately:

**30–40%**

Keep anchors:

**2–3 meaningful sets**

at reasonably heavy loads.

Finisher:

- optional 5-minute benchmark;
- or remove if recovery is poor.

# 10. Anchor Progression

Anchor progression is primarily **performance retention with optional improvement**.

Example:

Week 1:

Hack Squat — 150 kg × 6 @ 2 RIR

Week 5:

150 kg × 6 @ 1–2 RIR

This is successful retention.

If the athlete reaches:

**all prescribed sets at the top of the rep range with ≤2 RIR**

increase load next exposure.

Typical increases:

- upper body: +1–2.5 kg where available
- lower body: +2.5–5 kg

Do not force progression if calorie-deficit recovery is limiting.

---

# 11. Burn-Block Progression

Use a hybrid of:

1. double progression;
2. density progression.

Example:

Incline DB Press 2×6–10 + Chest-Supported Row 2×6–10.

Progression priority:

### First
Improve reps until top of range.

### Then
Increase load.

### Independently
Track time required to complete the prescribed block.

If load/reps remain equal and completion time improves with similar technique/RIR:

**density improved.**

Do not shorten rest below the point where target reps deteriorate badly.

---

# 12. Density Tracking

HyperPlanner should track density at the **block level**, not simply total workout duration.

Example:

```text
Burn Pair A
Week 1: 8:42
Week 4: 8:05
Same load/reps
Density: +7.6%
```

Potential simple metric:

```text
completed prescribed reps / elapsed block time
```

More advanced version:

```text
total external-load volume / block time
```

However, cross-exercise tonnage comparisons can be misleading.

For user-facing UI, favor:

- completion time;
- reps completed;
- load;
- improvement percentage for the same block.

---

# 13. REDLINE SCORE

Dashboard should show two different adaptations.

## RETENTION

Representative performance relative to baseline:

- Lower
- Push
- Pull
- Hinge

Example:

```text
LOWER      99%
PUSH       97%
PULL      102%
HINGE      98%
```

## DENSITY

Improvement in standardized Burn blocks.

Example:

```text
WORK DENSITY
+11%
```

Do not combine them into one fake physiological score unless the formula is clearly game-like.

The two metrics represent separate goals:

> **Keep strength. Increase work rate.**

---

# 14. Finisher Progression

Finishers progress through **performance inside a fixed time window**.

Track:

- completed quality rounds;
- distance;
- total reps;
- implement load.

Priority order:

1. complete the work cleanly;
2. increase rounds/reps;
3. slightly increase load;
4. never extend beyond the week's time cap.

Do not calculate or display exact calories burned from a finisher.

Energy expenditure cannot be estimated accurately enough from these workout logs to justify fake precision.

---

# 15. Finisher Safety / Failure Rules

Finishers should terminate early if:

- technique deteriorates substantially;
- dizziness/nausea prevents controlled work;
- loaded carries become unsafe;
- swing mechanics break down;
- pain appears.

Avoid technically demanding movements under fatigue such as:

- heavy barbell complexes;
- high-rep Olympic lifts;
- high-rep deadlifts;
- high-rep barbell squats;
- repeated heavy overhead work.

REDLINE should be brutal through **density**, not recklessness.

---

# 16. Exercise Swap Philosophy

A swap should preserve the role of the exercise.

## Anchor swaps

Preserve:

- movement family;
- heavy loading potential;
- similar systemic demand.

Examples:

Hack Squat ↔ Smith Squat ↔ High-Bar Squat

Weighted Pull-Up ↔ Heavy Pulldown

RDL ↔ Smith RDL ↔ Hip-Supported Heavy DB Deadlift

Bench ↔ OHP only if intentionally changing the program's primary press emphasis.

## Burn swaps

Preserve:

- target muscle;
- stability;
- setup speed;
- rep range;
- compatibility with paired exercise.

Example:

Chest-Supported Row ↔ Hammer Row is good.

Chest-Supported Row ↔ unsupported Pendlay Row is poor for a density block because systemic demand changes dramatically.

## Finisher swaps

Preserve:

- simple execution;
- large-muscle involvement;
- low setup time;
- safe repeatability under fatigue.

- dumbbell;

Optional substitutions:

### If sled exists

### If bike exists
Use short bike intervals.

### If rower exists

---

# 18. Session-Time Guardrail

Target:

**40–50 minutes**

Recommended rough allocation:

- Anchor: 10–15 min
- Burn work: 20–25 min
- Small work: 3–5 min
- Finisher: 5–8 min

If sessions routinely exceed 50–55 minutes:

1. inspect transition/setup time;
2. reduce the lowest-priority isolation set;
3. simplify exercise swaps;
4. do not reduce Anchor rest just to make the clock work.

---

# 19. Cardio Outside REDLINE

REDLINE itself should not prescribe large amounts of mandatory additional conditioning.

Optional low-intensity activity can remain separate from the lifting plan.

The program's identity is:

**high-density resistance training + brief finishers**

not:

**turn every day into HIIT.**

---

# 20. Recovery Logic

Because the user is assumed to be in a calorie deficit, HyperPlanner should be more conservative about fatigue escalation.

Potential recovery prompt after each session:

```text
How did today's REDLINE feel?

GREEN — Hard but controlled
AMBER — Performance noticeably down
RED — Could not recover / major performance collapse
```

Possible response:

### GREEN
Continue normally.

### AMBER twice within 7 days
Hold progression and reduce next finisher by 1–2 minutes.

### RED
Next session:
- no finisher;
- Burn blocks stay 2–3 RIR;
- Anchor remains submaximal.

This should remain simple and should not become Super Mutant-style dynamic workout generation.

---

# 21. Performance-Drop Trigger

If an Anchor falls approximately:

**>7.5–10% below baseline performance**

for two exposures without an obvious exercise change:

flag:

> **Performance under pressure**

Suggested response:

- maintain load rather than increase;
- reduce one Burn set;
- temporarily shorten/skip finisher;
- reassess after the next exposure.

Do not automatically interpret strength loss as muscle loss.

# 25. What REDLINE Is Not

Do not allow the design to drift into:

- circuit training with tiny weights;
- 20-rep barbell compounds;
- 20-minute finishers;
- mandatory failure on compounds;
- huge weekly set counts;
- exercise randomization;
- CrossFit-style technical work under fatigue;
- fake calorie calculations.

Its identity depends on the contrast:

### HEAVY ANCHOR
Slow enough to perform properly.

### DENSE BURN
Fast, efficient resistance work.

### SHORT FINISHER
High whole-body output.

# 26. App-Native Mechanics

REDLINE should have four signature systems.

## 1. Anchor Retention
Compare important strength markers against Week 1.

## 2. Burn-Block Density
Track how quickly standardized work is completed at similar performance.

## 3. Finisher Benchmark
Track quality rounds/reps/distance within a fixed time.

## 4. Fatigue Guardrail
If performance collapses, reduce density stress before reducing heavy-load exposure.

These are enough to make REDLINE feel like a HyperPlanner program rather than a static cutting routine.

# 28. Decisions Considered Locked

- Name: **REDLINE**
- 8 weeks
- 4 days/week
- Full Body ×4
- ~40–50 min sessions
- 12–14 conventional working sets/session
- one heavy Anchor each session
- high-density paired resistance work
- 5–8 minute finishers
- finisher time never exceeds 8 minutes
- whole-body balanced, not glute/lat specialized
- strength retention is a success metric
- density improvement is a second success metric
- no fake calorie-burn estimates

# 30. Guiding Principle

> **REDLINE is a strength-retention plan disguised as a metabolic assault.**

The athlete should leave each workout feeling that a lot happened in very little time, while the app quietly ensures that the loads and performance that matter are not disappearing during the cut.
